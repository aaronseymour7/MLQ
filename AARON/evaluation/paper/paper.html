Certified Stetcu–Baroni Ground-State Filtering from DMRG Trial States: Rigorous Bounds, a Hybrid Empirical Protocol, and a Resource Comparison for the J1–J2 Chain

Draft prepared from the AARON/j1j2_filter code base (internal evaluation; authors to be added)

6 October 2026

Abstract

Ground states of one-dimensional spin chains are routinely obtained classically by DMRG; a hybrid quantum workflow compiles the DMRG matrix-product state (MPS) into a shallow circuit and repairs its residual error with the ancilla-based projection algorithm of Stetcu, Baroni and Carlson. We study this workflow for the open spin-½ J1J_1–J2J_2 chain with a classically optimised, certified product-of-cosines filter and first-order Lie–Trotter evolution, and ask three questions: is the error analysis correct, how conservative is it, and does the filter beat simply preparing the MPS better? (1) Correctness. We re-derive the analysis and test every building block against brute force (pulse convention, spectral-suppression certificates, leakage bound, commutator constant, composed bound); no violation occurred in any test, and we prove a sharpened angle-based composition that tightens the bound by 2.5–3.1× and the a-priori step count by 2.3–3.2×. (2) Conservatism. The rigorous step count is 17–1200× the oracle-empirical one. We trace this to the worst-case commutator constant: the ground state feels a first-order Trotter coefficient 28–41× below it at J2=0J_2=0 (9–13× at J2=0.4J_2=0.4), a gap that widens with NN, plus 4× pulse-level and 2× composition slack. We propose a hybrid protocol — rigorous leakage certificate plus a Richardson-type empirical Trotter estimate that does not use the exact ground state — which met its infidelity target in every completed case (N=6N=6–1212) at roughly 30–600× lower CX cost than the certified circuits. (3) Resource comparison. Against the mps-to-circuit approximate and sequential (exact) MPS circuits and generic state preparation, the best baseline is cheaper than the certified filter by 10210^{2}–105×10^{5}\times and than the hybrid protocol by 5–74× for all N≤12N\le12 and J2∈{0,0.4}J_2\in\{0,0.4\}; MPS preparation needs only a bond dimension of 4–8 there, so these systems are too easy for the filter to offer an advantage. We therefore frame the contribution as a certified-filtering methodology and verification harness with an honest negative resource result for 1D chains at N≤12N\le12, identify the regimes (large bond dimension, higher dimension, large NN with tensor-network simulation of the filter) in which an advantage could still appear, and report the defects found in the reference implementation (a γ=1\gamma=1 fallback that built million-CX filters for exact states, an unguarded first-order assumption, an unreachable-memory dense check), all now fixed. The numbers in §5.8–5.10 are regenerated from the raw result files as further runs complete (paper/build_paper.py).

1. Introduction

Ground-state preparation is the entry point of most quantum simulation workflows. For one-dimensional gapped or critical spin chains, DMRG supplies classically excellent but not exactly circuit-preparable states; a natural hybrid is to compile the MPS into a low-depth circuit with fidelity γ<1\gamma<1 and repair the remainder with a quantum projection step. The Stetcu–Baroni–Carlson (SBC) projection algorithm [1] does this with one ancilla, controlled time evolution and post-selection; its randomised-time variant is the rodeo algorithm [2,3]. Here the pulse times and phases are instead optimised and certified classically, and the Trotter error is controlled by a commutator bound [4].

This paper evaluates that workflow as implemented in AARON/j1j2_filter and turns the evaluation into a methodology. It aims to state precisely what is established, what is conditional, and what the filter costs relative to the classical-quantum alternatives, supported by reproducible experiments (AARON/evaluation/experiments, results in AARON/evaluation/results). Negative results are reported as such; they motivate the hybrid protocol of §5.10 and the framing of §7.

Contributions. (i) A self-contained derivation of the error analysis, with a proven sharpened composition bound (§3). (ii) Independent verification of each analytic component and of the end-to-end bound (§5.1–5.2). (iii) A quantitative account of why the certified guarantee is 100–1000× above the empirical cost (§5.9). (iv) A non-oracle hybrid protocol that combines a rigorous leakage certificate with an empirical Trotter estimate (§5.10). (v) An equal-fidelity comparison with MPS-based and generic state preparation (§5.8). (vi) An assessment of the DMRG-derived inputs and of symmetry (§5.5–5.6). (vii) A code audit with fixes (§6).

2. Model, algorithm and conventions

Model. We study the open-boundary spin-½ J1J_1–J2J_2 chain H=∑i=1N−1J1𝐒i⋅𝐒i+1+∑i=1N−2J2𝐒i⋅𝐒i+2,𝐒i⋅𝐒j=14(XiXj+YiYj+ZiZj), H=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}J_1\,\mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_{i+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}J_2\,\mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_{i+2}, \qquad \mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_j=\tfrac14\left(X_iX_j+Y_iY_j+Z_iZ_j\right), with J1=1J_1=1, even NN (so that the ground state is a non-degenerate singlet) and J2∈{0,0.2411,0.5}J_2\in\{0,\,0.2411,\,0.5\} (Heisenberg, the Okamoto–Nomura dimerisation point, and the Majumdar–Ghosh point). The Pauli form has 3(N−1)+3(N−2)[J2≠0]3(N-1)+3(N-2)\,[J_2\neq0] terms. HH conserves total spin 𝐒2\mathbf S^2 and spatial reflection; the lowest excitation is a triplet.

Relation to prior work. The pulse is the deterministic-time, optimised-phase version of the projection/“rodeo” circuit of Stetcu, Baroni and Carlson (Phys. Rev. C 105, 064308 (2022)); with ϕi=0\phi_i=0 and random tit_i it reduces to the rodeo algorithm. The product-of-cosines filter is a real trigonometric polynomial in EE of total frequency TT and is therefore a restricted member of the family realised by quantum eigenvalue transformation of unitaries / QSVT (Lin–Tong, Dong–Lin–Tong), which achieve T=O(Δ−1log(1/η))T=O(\Delta^{-1}\log(1/\eta)) with a single ancilla but need coherent (not measurement-based) control. The present scheme trades that for a classically optimised filter that can be certified and for early-abort statistics.

The workflow in AARON/j1j2_filter has five stages. For each, we record what the code does, what it certifies and what it merely assumes.

4.1 Where DMRG enters, and what depends on it

  1. Trial state. The MPS is compiled to an LL-layer circuit. LL controls γ\gamma and the circuit cost; the filter exists to repair 1−γ1-\gamma.
  2. Spectral inputs. In the default configuration (SPECTRUM_SOURCE="ed") no DMRG number enters the certificate: E0,E1,EtopE_0,E_1,E_{\mathrm{top}} come from dense ED and DMRG is used only for the trial circuit and as a cross-check (ed_cross_check). This restricts every certified result to N≤12N\le12, where DMRG is not needed. With SPECTRUM_SOURCE="dmrg" the scaled window [Δ,1][\Delta,1] is built from DMRG quantities, and the guarantees of §3 become conditional on three assumptions that DMRG cannot certify:
    • (A1) E1DMRG≤E1E_1^{\mathrm{DMRG}}\le E_1 (so that Δ\Delta is a lower bound on the true gap). The penalty method returns an energy of a state orthogonal to an approximate |ψ0⟩|\psi_0\rangle, i.e. an upper bound on E1E_1 when ψ0\psi_0 is exact; the wrong side for a certificate (§6.4 quantifies the consequence).
    • (A2) γ\gamma is a lower bound. A DMRG–circuit overlap is an overlap with an approximation of |E0⟩|E_0\rangle.
    • (A3) the scaled ground energy is within e0e_0 of zero; get_spectrum sets e0=VarH/We_0=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}H}/W, which the code itself calls “indicative” (some eigenvalue lies within Var\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}} of ⟨H⟩\langle H\rangle, not necessarily the lowest).
    The bandwidth, in contrast, is certified: W=(cI+∑|cβ|)−E0DMRG≥Etop−E0DMRGW=(c_I+\sum|c_\beta|)-E_0^{\mathrm{DMRG}}\ge E_{\mathrm{top}}-E_0^{\mathrm{DMRG}}.
  3. Hilbert-space dimension. Every number in stage 5 (exact reference, Trotter simulation, trial-circuit statevector) is computed with dense 2N2^{N} vectors, so the pipeline never exploits DMRG’s ability to reach large NN. It is a verification harness for N≲14N\lesssim 14, not (yet) a large-NN workflow.

4.2 Filter design (“floor” problem)

For fixed (x=TΔ/π,m)(x{=}T\Delta/\pi,\,m) the solver (floor.solve_floor) maximises F(0)2F(0)^2 subject to certified η≤η*\eta\le\eta_* using SLSQP with analytic Jacobians, a ladder of feasibility guards, a cutting-plane refinement of the constraint grid and a certification fixed point. Across (x,m)(x,m) and leakage budgets εℓ=fε\varepsilon_\ell=f\varepsilon, f∈{0.4,…,0.01}f\in\{0.4,\dots,0.01\}, the pipeline picks the candidate minimising nreq/pglbn_{\mathrm{req}}/p_{g}^{\mathrm{lb}}. The continuous design is then snapped to nn equal steps (largest-remainder), zero-length pulses are dropped (a t=0t=0 pulse is a scalar cosϕ\cos\phi after post-selection, so η\eta and the state are unchanged), phases are re-optimised at the snapped times and η\eta is re-certified.

4.3 Two reported operating points

  • Guaranteed: smallest nn (5 % search) whose a-priori bound εbound≤ε\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bound}}\le\varepsilon.
  • Empirical: smallest nn (sweep + bisection) whose measured infidelity to the ED ground state is ≤ε\le\varepsilon.

The second is an oracle quantity (it uses the exact ground state that the algorithm is supposed to produce) and, as §6.2 shows, it can sit in a regime where the Trotterised filter is not an approximation of the designed filter at all.

All experiments use noiseless exact statevector (or exact-unitary) simulation, J1=1J_1=1, open boundaries, a one-layer (L=1L=1) brickwork trial circuit from the DMRG MPS unless stated, and the pipeline defaults (first-order Lie–Trotter, ED spectrum, floor design). Three observations.

(a) The guarantee is sound but loose. No design violated its bound. The measured infidelity at nguarn_{\mathrm{guar}} is 8–63× below the bound, and the sharpened composition (valid in every case) tightens the bound by 2.5–3.1× and reduces the a-priori nn by 2.3–3.2×. Two further sources of slack remain: the commutator norm is a worst case over the full Hilbert space (the pulse-level operator error is already only ≤0.24\le 0.24 of the bound, §5.1) and the state error on low-energy states is far smaller than the operator error (measured state error 2×10−42\times10^{-4} vs. bound 5.6×10−25.6\times10^{-2} at N=6N=6, ε=10−2\varepsilon=10^{-2}).

(b) The “empirical” point is an oracle and sometimes not a filter at all. nempn_{\mathrm{emp}} is defined by comparison with the exact ground state. In several rows (e.g. N=6N=6, ε=10−1,10−2\varepsilon=10^{-1},10^{-2}; N=8N=8, ε=10−1\varepsilon=10^{-1}) the certified η\eta at nempn_{\mathrm{emp}} is ≈1\approx1: the snapped Trotter step (dt≈3.6dt\approx3.6 in scaled units, i.e. Wdt≈13W\,dt\approx13 rad) is so coarse that the circuit is no longer an approximation of the designed filter, and the measured improvement is a property of that particular discretisation, not something a bound can certify or a user could know a priori. The empirical numbers should be presented as a lower envelope, not as the algorithm’s cost.

(c) Cost. The guaranteed circuits for N=6N=6 at ε=10−2\varepsilon=10^{-2} have 1450 Trotter steps, 50 765 CX, depth ≈2×105\approx2\times10^{5} and ≈7×104\approx7\times10^{4} non-Clifford rotations, i.e. ∼1.4×106\sim1.4\times10^{6} T gates under the code’s own synthesis estimate, with success probability Psucc≈0.72P_{\mathrm{succ}}\approx0.72.

5.3 Is the filter worth it? Baselines

5.6 Over-estimating the gap, and what symmetry could buy

5.10 A hybrid, non-oracle protocol: rigorous leakage plus empirical Trotter control

The “empirical” step counts of §5.2 use the exact ground state and are not available to a user. We therefore replace them by a protocol that uses only quantities a practitioner can obtain:

  1. Leakage — rigorous. For the snapped step grid, certify η\eta (R1) and compute the leakage bound ℓ(n)\ell(n) from R2 (needs γ\gamma and Δ\Delta).
  2. Trotter — empirical, Richardson-type. Run the same circuit family at nn and 2n2n and compute δn=1−|⟨ψn|ψ2n⟩|2\delta_n=1-|\langle\psi_n|\psi_{2n}\rangle|^2; because the error vector scales as 1/n1/n, the distance to the n→∞n\to\infty state is ≈2δn\approx2\sqrt{\delta_n}.
  3. Accept the smallest nn on a geometric grid with (ℓ(n)+2δn)2≤ε(\sqrt{\ell(n)}+2\sqrt{\delta_n})^2\le\varepsilon and certified η<1\eta<1.

The exact ground state is used only afterwards, to validate. (On hardware the two-run comparison is replaced by the convergence of an energy or other observable estimated at nn and 2n2n.) 6.1 Methodological improvements

  1. Sharpened composition (item 10): a free, proven 2.5–3.1× tighter bound and 2.3–3.2× fewer guaranteed steps.
  2. Symmetry-resolved certification: let wsw_s be the trial weight in sector ss and Δs\Delta_s the gap of that sector. Then ℓ≤∑sws(sup[Δs,1]|F|)2/F(0)2\ell\le\sum_sw_s\,(\sup_{[\Delta_s,1]}|F|)^2/F(0)^2 over the ground-state sector and the (small) remainder bounded with the global gap. Because bond-ordered Trotter steps are exactly SU(2)-symmetric, the sector weights are conserved by the (Trotterised) evolution, so this is rigorous given the sector weights, which are cheap to compute from the trial MPS. Potential saving up to (Δs/Δ)2≈7×(\Delta_s/\Delta)^2\approx7\times in nn at J2=0J_2=0 (§5.6).
  3. Subspace-restricted Trotter bounds: replace the global α\alpha by commutator norms restricted to the low-energy subspace populated by the trial state (the observed state error is ≈ 300× below the bound), or use higher-order formulas with the matching commutator bound.
  4. Tensor-network simulation of the filter. The DMRG advantage is lost as long as every number comes from dense vectors; the controlled evolution e−itH⊗Ze^{-itH\otimes Z} is an MPO-friendly operation, so MPS/TEBD simulation of the Trotterised filter would allow N∼30N\sim30–100100 and show the real DMRG→circuit→filter story.

7. Conclusions and framing

Established. (a) The mathematics of the filter, leakage, Trotter and composition bounds is correct and held in every test; a sharpened composition is proved and verified. (b) The certified guarantee is loose by a factor ∼102\sim10^{2}–10310^{3} because the commutator constant is a worst case over the whole Hilbert space; the slack is quantified (§5.9) and grows with NN. (c) A hybrid protocol — rigorous leakage plus a Richardson-type Trotter estimate, with no oracle — met its target in every completed case at 30–600× lower CX than the certified circuits. (d) DMRG supplies E0E_0, E1E_1, $E_{\rm top}$ and the ground state to ∼10−9\sim10^{-9} up to N=16N=16, but cannot certify the gap or γ\gamma.

Negative result. For the open J1J_1–J2J_2 chain at N≤12N\le12 and J2∈{0,0.4}J_2\in\{0,0.4\} the filter does not beat classical-then-quantum MPS preparation: the best baseline is cheaper than the certified filter by 10210^{2}–105×10^{5}\times and than the hybrid protocol by 5–74×, because a bond dimension of 4–8 already prepares these states to ≲10−2\lesssim10^{-2}–10−510^{-5} with a few hundred CX. The filter’s cost is set by the physical gap (T∝1/ΔT\propto1/\Delta) and Trotter error, not by how entangled the state is, so its relative merit can only improve for states whose MPS preparation cost grows quickly — which none of the tested ones do.

Framing. We therefore recommend presenting this work as (i) a methodology for certified SBC filtering (rigorous bounds, sharpened composition, symmetry-resolved certification, and the hybrid protocol), (ii) a verification harness with open code, and (iii) an honest comparison showing that for 1D chains of this size MPS preparation suffices, with the regimes where the filter might help named explicitly. It should not claim a resource advantage.

What would turn the negative result into a positive one (open work).

  1. States that are expensive for MPS circuits: two-dimensional or ladder lattices, critical systems at larger NN where χ\chi must grow, or trial states from methods other than DMRG. These require tensor-network or other large-NN simulation of the filter itself; dense vectors stop at N≈14N\approx14.
  2. Tighter and cheaper filtering: subspace-restricted Trotter bounds (the observed constant is 10–40× below α\alpha), second-order formulas with the matching commutator bound, symmetry-resolved windows (up to ≈7×\approx7\times in nn at J2=0J_2=0), absorbing the energy shift into the phases, and Hamiltonian-aware compilation of the controlled bond exponentials.
  3. Rigor for large NN: a certified lower bound on the gap (e.g. symmetry-resolved DMRG plus a Temple/Weinstein-type bound) so that Δ\Delta, γ\gamma and e0e_0 are not assumptions.
  4. Competing quantum methods under the same trial state: QETU/Lin–Tong filtering and the rodeo algorithm with random times.
  5. Completeness of the present data: the empirical-protocol sweep for N=10N=10–1212 at J2=0.4J_2=0.4 and N=12N=12 at J2=0J_2=0, ε=10−3\varepsilon=10^{-3} rows marked †, and L=3L=3 trial circuits; second-order Trotter; a fault-tolerant resource model with early-abort statistics; and verified references and T-count constants.
