Certified Ground-State Filtering with DMRG Trial States and Stetcu–Baroni Projection: An Evaluation of the J1–J2 Chain Workflow

Draft prepared from the AARON/j1j2_filter code base (internal evaluation; authors to be added)

6 October 2026

Abstract

We evaluate a workflow that prepares the ground state of the open spin-½ J1J_1–J2J_2 Heisenberg chain on a quantum computer by (i) running DMRG to obtain a matrix-product-state (MPS) approximation, (ii) compiling it into a shallow brickwork circuit, and (iii) refining that trial state with the ancilla-based projection algorithm of Stetcu, Baroni and Carlson, using a classically optimised, certified product-of-cosines filter and first-order Lie–Trotter evolution with a rigorous a-priori error bound. We re-derive the mathematics, test every analytic building block against brute force, and run the full pipeline for N=4N=4–1212 sites. Findings. (1) The analytic ingredients are sound: pulse convention, spectral-suppression certificates, leakage bound, Trotter commutator constant and the composed error bound showed no violation in any test (0 of 60 certificate designs, 0 of 2×1042\times10^4 random spectra, 0 of 5×1045\times10^4 random state pairs, and every simulated design). (2) The composed bound is conservative by a large factor: at the guaranteed step count the measured infidelity is 8–60× below the bound, and the guaranteed step number is 17–1200× the oracle-empirical one. A sharpened angle-based composition we prove tightens the bound by 2.5–3.1× and lowers the a-priori step count by 2.3–3.2×. (3) The guaranteed cost scales as n∝N4n\propto N^{4} and CX ∝N5\propto N^{5} at fixed target; for N=6N=6–1010 it is 700–1000× the CX count of exact state preparation (57–1013 CX) and orders of magnitude above shallow MPS circuits, so the current evidence does not support a resource advantage. (4) Where DMRG matters for certification it cannot supply the needed quantities: in the default configuration the certified spectrum comes from exact diagonalisation (N≤12N\le12), and the DMRG excited-state energy errs on the unsafe side for a gap lower bound. (5) The J2=0.5J_2=0.5 benchmark is trivial (Majumdar–Ghosh dimer state, γ=1\gamma=1) and exposes a code path that builds a million-CX filter for a state that is already exact. We list the code defects found, and the experiments and proofs that a publishable version would still need.

1. Introduction

Ground-state preparation is the entry point of most quantum simulation workflows. For one-dimensional gapped or critical spin chains, DMRG supplies classically excellent but not exactly circuit-preparable states; a natural hybrid is to compile the MPS into a low-depth circuit with fidelity γ<1\gamma<1 and repair the remainder with a quantum projection step. The Stetcu–Baroni–Carlson (SBC) projection algorithm [1] does this with one ancilla, controlled time evolution and post-selection; its randomised-time variant is the rodeo algorithm [2,3]. Here the pulse times and phases are instead optimised and certified classically, and the Trotter error is controlled by a commutator bound [4].

This document is an independent evaluation of that workflow as implemented in AARON/j1j2_filter. Its aim is to state precisely what has been established, what is conditional, and what is still missing for a publishable paper, supported by reproducible experiments (AARON/evaluation/experiments, results in AARON/evaluation/results). We deliberately report negative as well as positive findings; the conclusions in §7 are written so they can be carried into a manuscript unchanged once the open items are closed.

Contributions. (i) A self-contained derivation of the error analysis, including a sharpened composition bound (Proposition in §3). (ii) Independent verification of each analytic component and of the end-to-end bound. (iii) Quantitative comparison of the guaranteed and empirical resource counts and of the scaling with NN. (iv) A baseline comparison with shallow MPS circuits and with exact state preparation. (v) An assessment of the DMRG-derived inputs and of the effect of symmetry. Model. We study the open-boundary spin-½ J1J_1–J2J_2 chain H=∑i=1N−1J1𝐒i⋅𝐒i+1+∑i=1N−2J2𝐒i⋅𝐒i+2,𝐒i⋅𝐒j=14(XiXj+YiYj+ZiZj), H=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}J_1\,\mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_{i+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}J_2\,\mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_{i+2}, \qquad \mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_j=\tfrac14\left(X_iX_j+Y_iY_j+Z_iZ_j\right), with J1=1J_1=1, even NN (so that the ground state is a non-degenerate singlet) and J2∈{0,0.2411,0.5}J_2\in\{0,\,0.2411,\,0.5\} (Heisenberg, the Okamoto–Nomura dimerisation point, and the Majumdar–Ghosh point). Relation to prior work. The pulse is the deterministic-time, optimised-phase version of the projection/“rodeo” circuit of Stetcu, Baroni and Carlson (Phys. Rev. C 105, 064308 (2022)); with ϕi=0\phi_i=0 and random tit_i it reduces to the rodeo algorithm. The present scheme trades that for a classically optimised filter that can be certified and for early-abort statistics.

4.1 Where DMRG enters, and what depends on it

  1. Trial state. The MPS is compiled to an LL-layer circuit. LL controls γ\gamma and the circuit cost; the filter exists to repair 1−γ1-\gamma.
  2. Spectral inputs. In the default configuration (SPECTRUM_SOURCE="ed") no DMRG number enters the certificate: E0,E1,EtopE_0,E_1,E_{\mathrm{top}} come from dense ED and DMRG is used only for the trial circuit and as a cross-check (ed_cross_check). This restricts every certified result to N≤12N\le12, where DMRG is not needed. With SPECTRUM_SOURCE="dmrg" the scaled window [Δ,1][\Delta,1] is built from DMRG quantities, and the guarantees of §3 become conditional on three assumptions that DMRG cannot certify:
    • (A1) E1DMRG≤E1E_1^{\mathrm{DMRG}}\le E_1 (so that Δ\Delta is a lower bound on the true gap). The penalty method returns an energy of a state orthogonal to an approximate |ψ0⟩|\psi_0\rangle, i.e. an upper bound on E1E_1 when ψ0\psi_0 is exact; the wrong side for a certificate (§6.4 quantifies the consequence).
    • (A2) γ\gamma is a lower bound. A DMRG–circuit overlap is an overlap with an approximation of |E0⟩|E_0\rangle.
    • (A3) the scaled ground energy is within e0e_0 of zero; get_spectrum sets e0=VarH/We_0=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}H}/W, which the code itself calls “indicative” (some eigenvalue lies within Var\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}} of ⟨H⟩\langle H\rangle, not necessarily the lowest).
    The bandwidth, in contrast, is certified: W=(cI+∑|cβ|)−E0DMRG≥Etop−E0DMRGW=(c_I+\sum|c_\beta|)-E_0^{\mathrm{DMRG}}\ge E_{\mathrm{top}}-E_0^{\mathrm{DMRG}}.
  3. Hilbert-space dimension. Every number in stage 5 (exact reference, Trotter simulation, trial-circuit statevector) is computed with dense 2N2^{N} vectors, so the pipeline never exploits DMRG’s ability to reach large NN. It is a verification harness for N≲14N\lesssim 14, not (yet) a large-NN workflow.

4.2 Filter design (“floor” problem)

For fixed (x=TΔ/π,m)(x{=}T\Delta/\pi,\,m) the solver (floor.solve_floor) maximises F(0)2F(0)^2 subject to certified η≤η*\eta\le\eta_* using SLSQP with analytic Jacobians, a ladder of feasibility guards, a cutting-plane refinement of the constraint grid and a certification fixed point. Across (x,m)(x,m) and leakage budgets εℓ=fε\varepsilon_\ell=f\varepsilon, f∈{0.4,…,0.01}f\in\{0.4,\dots,0.01\}, the pipeline picks the candidate minimising nreq/pglbn_{\mathrm{req}}/p_{g}^{\mathrm{lb}}. The continuous design is then snapped to nn equal steps (largest-remainder), zero-length pulses are dropped (a t=0t=0 pulse is a scalar cosϕ\cos\phi after post-selection, so η\eta and the state are unchanged), phases are re-optimised at the snapped times and η\eta is re-certified.

4.3 Two reported operating points

  • Guaranteed: smallest nn (5 % search) whose a-priori bound εbound≤ε\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bound}}\le\varepsilon.
  • Empirical: smallest nn (sweep + bisection) whose measured infidelity to the ED ground state is ≤ε\le\varepsilon.

The second is an oracle quantity (it uses the exact ground state that the algorithm is supposed to produce) and, as §6.2 shows, it can sit in a regime where the Trotterised filter is not an approximation of the designed filter at all. ## 5. Verification and results

Three observations.

(a) The guarantee is sound but loose. No design violated its bound. The measured infidelity at nguarn_{\mathrm{guar}} is 8–63× below the bound, and the sharpened composition (valid in every case) tightens the bound by 2.5–3.1× and reduces the a-priori nn by 2.3–3.2×. Two further sources of slack remain: the commutator norm is a worst case over the full Hilbert space (the pulse-level operator error is already only ≤0.24\le 0.24 of the bound, §5.1) and the state error on low-energy states is far smaller than the operator error (measured state error 2×10−42\times10^{-4} vs. bound 5.6×10−25.6\times10^{-2} at N=6N=6, ε=10−2\varepsilon=10^{-2}).

(b) The “empirical” point is an oracle and sometimes not a filter at all. nempn_{\mathrm{emp}} is defined by comparison with the exact ground state. In several rows (e.g. N=6N=6, ε=10−1,10−2\varepsilon=10^{-1},10^{-2}; N=8N=8, ε=10−1\varepsilon=10^{-1}) the certified η\eta at nempn_{\mathrm{emp}} is ≈1\approx1: the snapped Trotter step (dt≈3.6dt\approx3.6 in scaled units, i.e. Wdt≈13W\,dt\approx13 rad) is so coarse that the circuit is no longer an approximation of the designed filter, and the measured improvement is a property of that particular discretisation, not something a bound can certify or a user could know a priori. The empirical numbers should be presented as a lower envelope, not as the algorithm’s cost.

(c) Cost. The guaranteed circuits for N=6N=6 at ε=10−2\varepsilon=10^{-2} have 1450 Trotter steps, 50 765 CX, depth ≈2×105\approx2\times10^{5} and ≈7×104\approx7\times10^{4} non-Clifford rotations, i.e. ∼1.4×106\sim1.4\times10^{6} T gates under the code’s own synthesis estimate, with success probability Psucc≈0.72P_{\mathrm{succ}}\approx0.72.

5.3 Is the filter worth it? Baselines

Exact preparation of the ED ground state with qiskit’s generic isometry synthesis costs 57, 247, 1013 and 4083 CX for N=6,8,10,12N=6,8,10,12 (≈2N\approx2^{N}). The guaranteed filter at ε=10−2\varepsilon=10^{-2} costs 50 765, 240 268 and 696 807 CX for N=6,8,10N=6,8,10, i.e. 890×, 970× and 690× more than exact preparation, and 2–3 orders of magnitude more than the entire L≤8L\le8 MPS-circuit family (15–264 CX). The MPS-circuit baseline is itself imperfect and non-monotone in LL (e.g. N=6N=6: infidelity 1.4×10−21.4\times10^{-2} at L=3L=3, 1.8×10−21.8\times10^{-2} at L=4L=4; the compilation is a local optimisation), and at N=10,12N=10,12 it has not converged by L=8L=8 (5.8 % and 12 % infidelity). Extrapolating the fitted N5N^5 filter cost against the 2N2^N exact cost suggests a crossover near N≈26N\approx26 — but dense isometry synthesis is not available at that size and the extrapolation ignores the decay of γ\gamma with NN; the relevant comparators there are MPS-based sequential preparation [7] and QETU/Lin–Tong filters [5,6]. This comparison is the most important missing piece of the present study.

5.4 Scaling with system size

Guaranteed nn for N=4,6,8,10N=4,6,8,10: 226, 1523, 4903, 11 060 (fit exponent 4.3 over 4–10, 3.9 over 6–10); CX: 4.8×1034.8\times10^3, 5.3×1045.3\times10^4, 2.4×1052.4\times10^5, 7.0×1057.0\times10^5 (exponent 5.0–5.5). Using L=3L=3 trial layers raises γ\gamma (0.98, 0.94, 0.90 for N=6,8,10N=6,8,10) and lowers guaranteed CX by 3.2×, 1.7× and 1.5× respectively: the benefit of a better trial state shrinks with NN because the evolution time T∝1/ΔgapT\propto1/\Delta_{\mathrm{gap}} is set by the gap, not by γ\gamma.

5.6 Over-estimating the gap, and what symmetry could buy

We designed filters for a gap over-estimated by s∈{−20%,…,+100%}s\in\{-20\%,\dots,+100\%\} (N=6N=6, J2=0J_2=0, εℓ=10−3\varepsilon_\ell=10^{-3}) and evaluated them on the true spectrum.

The certificate fails as soon as the design gap exceeds the true one: certified η\eta on the true window rises from 0.092 to 0.15, 0.21, 0.28, 0.34 and 0.50 for s=10,20,35,50,100%s=10,20,35,50,100\% (design target 0.092). The measured leakage of this particular trial state nevertheless stays below the 10−310^{-3} target (≤7×10−4\le7\times10^{-4}). The reason is symmetry: the L=1L=1 trial state has 96.3 % of its weight in S=0S=0, 0.49 % in S=1S=1, 3.2 % in S=2S=2, and the triplet that fixes the gap carries almost none of it, while the first S=0S=0 excitation lies at 2.66× the triplet gap (N=6N=6, J2=0J_2=0). A rigorous statement therefore needs the sector weights, which suggests a concrete improvement (§6): because every bond exponential is SU(2)-symmetric (“bond” ordering) the Trotterised evolution preserves SS, so one may certify with sector-dependent windows. 5.7 The J2=0.5J_2=0.5 case is trivial, and exposes a defect

At the Majumdar–Ghosh point the open even chain has the product-of-dimers ground state [8], which a single brickwork layer reproduces exactly: we find γ=1\gamma=1 (to double precision) for N=4N=4–1212. floor_vs_precision then correctly returns “no filter needed” rows (m=0m=0), but choose_floor_design counts them as infeasible and make_design silently falls back to the legacy builder design with a hard-coded Psucc≥0.9P_{\mathrm{succ}}\ge0.9. The pipeline then reports guaranteed circuits of 24 261, 511 068 and 1 157 409 CX for N=4,8,10N=4,8,10 to prepare a state it already has, and the oracle-empirical search “meets” ε=0.1\varepsilon=0.1 with a state that has lost 9 % fidelity. The near-MG regime is therefore a poor benchmark for the algorithm; the interesting frustrated regime is J2∼0.3J_2\sim0.3–0.450.45 or non-dimerised trial limits (small LL at larger NN). ## 6. 6.1 Methodological improvements that follow from the analysis

  1. Sharpened composition (item 10): a free, proven 2.5–3.1× tighter bound and 2.3–3.2× fewer guaranteed steps.
  2. Symmetry-resolved certification: let wsw_s be the trial weight in sector ss and Δs\Delta_s the gap of that sector. Then ℓ≤∑sws(sup[Δs,1]|F|)2/F(0)2\ell\le\sum_sw_s\,(\sup_{[\Delta_s,1]}|F|)^2/F(0)^2 over the ground-state sector and the (small) remainder bounded with the global gap. Because bond-ordered Trotter steps are exactly SU(2)-symmetric, the sector weights are conserved by the (Trotterised) evolution, so this is rigorous given the sector weights, which are cheap to compute from the trial MPS. Potential saving up to (Δs/Δ)2≈7×(\Delta_s/\Delta)^2\approx7\times in nn at J2=0J_2=0 (§5.6).
  3. Subspace-restricted Trotter bounds: replace the global α\alpha by commutator norms restricted to the low-energy subspace populated by the trial state (the observed state error is ≈ 300× below the bound), or use higher-order formulas with the matching commutator bound.
  4. Tensor-network simulation of the filter. The DMRG advantage is lost as long as every number comes from dense vectors; the controlled evolution e−itH⊗Ze^{-itH\otimes Z} is an MPO-friendly operation, so MPS/TEBD simulation of the Trotterised filter would allow N∼30N\sim30–100100 and show the real DMRG→circuit→filter story.

7. Conclusions and what a publishable paper still needs

Established. The mathematics of the filter, leakage, Trotter and composition bounds is correct, and verified numerically in every test performed; the pipeline’s reference implementation (pulse convention, MPO/Pauli agreement, circuit-vs-numpy agreement to 10−1610^{-16}) is consistent; DMRG supplies ground-state and excitation energies to ∼10−9\sim10^{-9} up to N=16N=16.

Not established. (i) A resource advantage: at N≤12N\le12 the guaranteed filter costs 10210^{2}–103×10^{3}\times exact preparation and ≫\gg shallow MPS circuits, and the empirical savings come from an oracle. (ii) A DMRG-specific role in certification: the certified numbers are ED-based. (iii) Anything at N>12N>12, near criticality or in the frustrated regime away from the exactly solvable MG point.

Required for submission (in priority order).

  1. Baselines. Compare resources at equal fidelity against (a) deeper/optimised MPS circuits and sequential MPS preparation, (b) QETU/Lin–Tong filtering with the same trial state, (c) rodeo with random times. Without this the paper cannot claim usefulness.
  2. Large-NN data from a tensor-network simulation of the filter (item 4 above), with γ(N,L)\gamma(N,L) and the gap from DMRG labelled as estimates, and a stated rigorous or heuristic gap input.
  3. Tighter guarantees: items 10 and 2 of §6.1 at minimum, to close the 100–1000× gap to the empirical cost, and an honest empirical definition (certified η<1\eta<1).
  4. Benchmarks that stress the algorithm: J2∈[0.2,0.45]J_2\in[0.2,0.45], N≥16N\ge16, and trial states with γ≪1\gamma\ll1; drop J2=0.5J_2=0.5 or use it only as a sanity check.
  5. Repository hygiene (items 1–5 of §6), a test suite, pinned environment, and archived raw data for every table and figure.
  6. Resource model: Hamiltonian-aware compilation of e−ithb⊗Ze^{-it\,h_b\otimes Z} (bond exponentials are SU(2)-symmetric), verified T-count constants, and a fault-tolerant estimate with early-abort statistics.

Suggested framing if the above is only partially achievable: a methods paper on certified SBC filtering — rigorous a-priori bounds, symmetry-resolved certification, and a verification harness — rather than a performance paper.
