Certified ground-state filtering from DMRG trial states — pipeline overview

J1–J2 Heisenberg chain · Stetcu–Baroni projection · what we built, what we proved, what it costs

0. One-paragraph summary

We want the ground state |E0⟩|E_0\rangle of the open spin-½ J1J_1–J2J_2 chain on a quantum computer. DMRG (classical) gives a matrix-product state (MPS) that we compile into a shallow circuit; that circuit has ground-state weight γ<1\gamma<1. We repair it with the Stetcu–Baroni–Carlson (SBC) projection: a single ancilla, controlled time evolution, and post-selection implement a filter F(E)=∏icos(Eti+ϕi)F(E)=\prod_i\cos(Et_i+\phi_i) that suppresses excited states. We optimise the filter classically, certify how well it suppresses the spectrum, and control the Trotter error three ways (certified, hybrid, oracle). Result: the mathematics is sound and verified, the rigorous guarantee is 10210^2–103×10^3\times more conservative than reality, a hybrid protocol recovers most of that gap — but for N≤12N\le12 an MPS-based circuit is still 5–74× cheaper than the filter, so no resource advantage on these 1D chains. The value is the certified-filtering method and its verification harness.

2. Stage by stage

Stage 1–2: Hamiltonian and DMRG (classical)

  • hamiltonians.py builds the Pauli form (for circuits and ED) and the MPO (for DMRG); check_mpo_matches_pauli verifies they agree to 10−910^{-9}.
  • get_energies.py runs two-site DMRG (bond dims {8,16,32,64,64}\{8,16,32,64,64\}, cutoff 10−1010^{-10}) for E0E_0 and the MPS $|\psi_{\rm DMRG}\rangle$; DMRG on −H-H gives $E_{\rm top}$; DMRG on H+λ|ψ0⟩⟨ψ0|H+\lambda|\psi_0\rangle\langle\psi_0| with $\lambda=1.1\,(E_{\rm top}-E_0)$ gives E1E_1 (penalty method).
  • Cross-check against exact diagonalisation (ED): DMRG matches $E_0,E_1,E_{\rm top}$ to ≲2×10−9\lesssim2\times10^{-9} up to N=16N=16.
  • Caveat that drives the certification discussion: DMRG gives estimates, not bounds. The penalty-method E1E_1 is variational (≥\ge true), i.e. on the unsafe side if used as a gap lower bound.

Stage 3: trial circuit

  • mps_to_circuit (qiskit-community mps-to-circuit) compiles the MPS to an LL-layer brickwork circuit of two-qubit isometries (method="approximate"); the "exact" method is a sequential (Schön et al.) preparation of an MPS with bond dimension χ\chi.
  • $\gamma=|\langle E_0|\psi_{\rm trial}\rangle|^2$ is the ground-state weight of the trial state. It is what the filter must repair (1−γ1-\gamma is the “error to remove”). On the certified path γ\gamma is computed against the ED ground state (exact for N≤12N\le12).

Stage 4: scaling the spectrum

$$H_s=\frac{H-E_0}{W},\qquad W\ge E_{\rm top}-E_0,\qquad \operatorname{spec}(H_s)\subset\{0\}\cup[\Delta,1],\quad \Delta=\frac{E_1-E_0}{W}.$$

All pulse times are in units of W−1W^{-1}. WW drops out of the Trotter step count (αT2\alpha T^2 is WW-independent), so the choice only affects how wide a window [Δ,1][\Delta,1] the filter must suppress. The code can take WW from ED (exact), or certify it as cI+∑|cβ|−E0c_I+\sum|c_\beta|-E_0 (valid but ≈3× too large because XX+YY+ZZXX{+}YY{+}ZZ is bounded term by term; the per-bond bound $E_{\rm top}\le\tfrac14[J_1(N-1)+J_2(N-2)]$ is tighter).

Stage 5: the filter (the core idea)

One pulse acts on the system and one ancilla (prepared in |0⟩|0\rangle):

$$\mathcal W(t,\phi)=\mathsf H_{\rm a}\;e^{-itH_s\otimes Z_{\rm a}}\;\mathrm{Rz}_{\rm a}(2\phi)\;\mathsf H_{\rm a},\qquad \langle0_{\rm a}|\mathcal W|0_{\rm a}\rangle=\cos(H_st+\phi).$$

Measuring the ancilla after each of mm pulses and keeping only outcome 0 applies

$$F(E)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\cos(Et_i+\phi_i)\quad\text{to the trial state},\qquad P_{\rm succ}=\sum_k|c_k|^2F(E_k)^2\;\ge\;\gamma\,F(0)^2 .$$

Stage 7: how do we choose nn and claim an error? — three ways

The final state differs from |E0⟩|E_0\rangle for two reasons:

  1. Leakage ℓ\ell — the (ideal, exact-evolution) filter does not remove excited states completely. R2: $F_{\rm exact}\ge\gamma/(\gamma+(1-\gamma)\eta^2)$, so ℓ≤(1−γ)η2/(γ+(1−γ)η2)\ell\le(1-\gamma)\eta^2/(\gamma+(1-\gamma)\eta^2). Rigorous, needs γ\gamma and Δ\Delta.
  2. Trotter error — time evolution is discretised. Worst-case theory (Childs et al.): ∥𝒲−𝒲̃∥≤αt2/2k\|\mathcal W-\widetilde{\mathcal W}\|\le\alpha t^2/2k, with α=∑g∥[Hg,∑g′>gHg′]∥\alpha=\sum_g\|[H_g,\sum_{g'>g}H_{g'}]\|; telescoping over pulses and renormalising gives ϵT=αT2/(2n)\epsilon_T=\alpha T^2/(2n) and a state distance dT≤2ϵT/pgd_T\le2\epsilon_T/\sqrt{p_g} with pg=γF(0)2p_g=\gamma F(0)^2.

Composition. Original code: 1−F≤(2ℓ+dT)21-F\le(\sqrt{2\ell}+d_T)^2. Our proven sharpened version: 1−F≤sin2(arcsinℓ+arcsin(ϵT/pg))1-F\le\sin^2\!\big(\arcsin\sqrt\ell+\arcsin(\epsilon_T/\sqrt{p_g})\big) — about 3× tighter (verified, 0 violations).

What “hybrid” means, precisely

Hybrid = rigorous for the part we can prove tightly, empirical for the part where the proof is too pessimistic. The leakage half is a theorem (certified η\eta). The Trotter half is replaced by a convergence test:

  1. Pick nn from a geometric grid; build the snapped design at nn and at 2n2n and run both circuits (classically now; on hardware, compare an observable such as energy at nn vs 2n2n).
  2. δn=1−|⟨ψn|ψ2n⟩|2\delta_n=1-|\langle\psi_n|\psi_{2n}\rangle|^2. Because the first-order error vector scales as 1/n1/n, ψn−ψ∞≈2(ψn−ψ2n)\psi_n-\psi_\infty\approx2(\psi_n-\psi_{2n}), so the distance to the converged state is ≈2δn\approx2\sqrt{\delta_n}.
  3. Accept the smallest nn with (ℓ(n)+2δn)2≤ε\big(\sqrt{\ell(n)}+2\sqrt{\delta_n}\big)^2\le\varepsilon and certified η<1\eta<1.

It never looks at the exact ground state (that is used afterwards only to validate). It is not a theorem: it assumes the asymptotic 1/n1/n regime. Why it is needed: the worst-case constant α\alpha is attained on high-energy states, but the state we care about feels a first-order coefficient 28–41× smaller (J2=0J_2=0; 9–13× at J2=0.4J_2=0.4), a gap that grows with NN (§4.2).

Pulse convention; R1 certificates (60 random designs vs brute force); R2 (2×1042\times10^4 random spectra); α\alpha vs dense commutator norms; pulse-level Trotter error (≤0.24×\le0.24\times bound); both composition bounds (5×1045\times10^4 random trials) — no violation anywhere, and every simulated design respected its bound.

4.5 DMRG’s actual role

DMRG accuracy is excellent (∼10−9\sim10^{-9} up to N=16N=16), but (i) in the certified path the spectral inputs come from ED (N≤12N\le12), (ii) DMRG’s E1E_1 is variational (unsafe for a gap lower bound), (iii) $e_0=\sqrt{\rm Var}/W$ is “indicative”, not a bound, and (iv) all downstream simulation uses dense vectors (N≲14N\lesssim14), so DMRG’s large-NN reach is unused. For large NN the guarantees are conditional on three assumptions: gap lower bound, γ\gamma lower bound, ground-energy offset.
